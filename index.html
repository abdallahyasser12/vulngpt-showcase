01 / THE APPROACH

A response is a clue.
Not proof.

Finding a suspicious response is easy. Establishing what it means — under the right identity, state, and permissions — is the hard part.

A / 01

Investigate context

Keep actors, sessions, object ownership, and application state in view instead of treating every request as an isolated payload.

A / 02

Challenge the hypothesis

Separate discovery from confirmation. Look for controlled evidence that supports — or falsifies — a suspected issue.

A / 03

Make results actionable

Prioritize reproducible evidence and clear reasoning, so security teams spend less time triaging unsupported signals.

05 / VALIDATION PRINCIPLE

Evidence before conclusions.

A security result should explain the expected boundary, the controlled test, the observed behavior, and the limitations. A suspicious signal is a reason to investigate—not a performance claim.
